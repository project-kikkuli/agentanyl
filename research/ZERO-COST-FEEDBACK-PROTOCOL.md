## Question and selection disclosure

The paid study completed eight sessions: the agent always selected the zero-fee route, with accurate task answers, message copying, route–message record interpretation and independent capability checks. That establishes no demonstrated one-point avoidance effect, but leaves a weaker preference compatible with the data. This follow-up removes the strict point advantage of either route while retaining the same stimuli and contingency control. It was selected after observing that paid result; it is not an independently preregistered replication.

Does the agent choose the nominal neutral-message route more often when its action controls the next message than when the same free-phase messages are replayed independently of its actions, **when both routes cost zero**?
